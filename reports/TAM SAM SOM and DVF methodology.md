# Make every market number earn its place

The muru-D agent's current skills point the right way, but three core rules reward the wrong things. The adoption term sits inside TAM. SOM is a share of someone else's market rather than the startup's own reach. A DVF score of 5 can come from well-sourced desk research, which the field's main evidence scale caps at 2. Rigorous practice rests on four habits. First, the core estimate is bottom-up: official counts of the buying unit times realized price, with top-down used only as a ceiling ([Stratrix](https://www.stratrix.com/craft/tam-sam-som)). Second, SOM is built from sales or channel capacity over a stated horizon. Third, every figure carries two separate grades, one for the source and one for the claim, plus the year it measures ([Wikipedia: Intelligence source and information reliability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_source_and_information_reliability)). Fourth, DVF scores are capped by evidence type, because what people say never counts as much as what they do ([Strategyzer](https://strategyzer.com/library/business-testing-is-your-hypothesis-really-validated)). The agent needs these rules because LLMs fail in known ways. Their nominal 99% intervals on Fermi problems contain the truth only about 65% of the time ([FermiEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26995)). In some tests, 23-47% of claims in AI search output had no support in the cited page ([DeepTRACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04499)). Most models also lean optimistic ([OptimismBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26981)). So the most valuable change is not better prompting. It is a per-number evidence ledger, with all arithmetic done in the code runner and a separate pass that re-fetches every citation. Together these let an analyst check any figure in seconds instead of rebuilding the spreadsheet.

## Bottom-up counts lead, and top-down only sets the ceiling

Working definitions mostly agree. TAM is annual revenue if every potential customer bought at a realistic price. SAM is the part your product, channels and geography can serve today. SOM is the part of SAM you can win **within a stated period** ([Stratrix](https://www.stratrix.com/craft/tam-sam-som)). Secondary sources blur SAM between "can serve" and "choose to target" ([Startup Savant](https://startupsavant.com/startup-marketing/understanding-tam-sam-and-som)), and no source sets a standard SOM horizon. The agent should define SAM strictly as servable today, keep the beachhead segment as its own named filter, and state SOM as year-3 revenue, not as a percentage. That horizon follows the one rigorous worked example found, not an industry rule.

The current skill gets the build direction right but puts adoption in the wrong place. In professional practice, bottom-up TAM is customers times units times **realized price**, with the unit matched to the count (seat, site or firm). Each SAM filter is applied one at a time with a stated reason, and the filters are kept mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive (MECE) ([Stratrix](https://www.stratrix.com/craft/tam-sam-som)). A buyers times adoption times price line mixes a SOM-stage judgement into TAM and hides it. Adoption belongs in SOM. Stratrix's worked example shows the discipline. It starts with 440,900 US technicians from official occupation data and ends at a TAM of about $359.8M. SAM, about $181M, comes from census counts of establishments with 10-99 employees. SOM is about $5.2M ARR after three years, roughly 2.9% of serviceable accounts. The same source shows that using the broadest industry code would have inflated TAM about **16x** ([Stratrix](https://www.stratrix.com/craft/tam-sam-som)).

New categories need a different starting point. In 2014 Damodaran sized Uber against the roughly $100B global taxi market. Gurley argued instead that cheaper, faster service would grow usage and replace some car ownership, which put the range at about **$450B to $1.3T** ([Gurley 2014](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)). Hindsight favours Gurley. Uber's Q4 2024 Mobility gross bookings were $22.8B, about a quarter of Damodaran's annual TAM in a single quarter, although bookings and taxi revenue are not the same measure ([Stratrix](https://www.stratrix.com/craft/tam-sam-som)). Stratrix's fix keeps both views honest: "TAM today" covers the spend being replaced, and a separate "Expansion TAM" line has its own thesis and its own test. The agent's "published total narrowed by filters" is a supply-side method. It under-sizes disruptive ideas and over-sizes niche ones, so it should only feed the ceiling.

For Australia, the ABS Counts of Australian Businesses (cat. 8165) is the default source of B2B buyer counts. At 30 June 2026 it showed **2,814,778 actively trading businesses, of which only 996,203 were employing**. The next release is due 18 December 2026 ([ABS 8165](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/business-indicators/counts-australian-businesses-including-entries-and-exits/latest-release)). An "SME" TAM that counts all 2.8M businesses therefore counts about 1.8M non-employing entities, most of them sole traders and holding structures. The default should be employing businesses by ANZSIC division and size band. In 2025-26 entries ran at 16.9% and exits at 13.8% ([ABS 8165](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/business-indicators/counts-australian-businesses-including-entries-and-exits/latest-release)). Business exits therefore set a structural floor under customer churn, and new businesses form a fresh acquisition pool every year. The ABS Data API needs no key and returns CSV ([ABS Data API guide](https://www.abs.gov.au/about/data-services/application-programming-interfaces-apis/data-api-user-guide)). Household spend is harder. The Household Expenditure Survey was last published for 2015-16 and has no scheduled successor ([ABS forward work program](https://www.abs.gov.au/about/our-organisation/corporate-reporting/abs-corporate-plan/2026-27/changes-forward-work-program)), so any consumer sizing built on it should carry low confidence. Commercial sources are mostly paywalled. A fetch of an IBISWorld Australia page returned no figures at all ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/au/industry/software-publishing/)). In practice, then, press-released summaries reach the agent as secondary sources.

## A credible SOM comes from the sales engine, not from a share of SAM

Stratrix puts it bluntly: "No standard SOM percentage exists." SOM should be built from sales capacity, win rate and churn, and then checked against the pipeline it requires ([Stratrix](https://www.stratrix.com/craft/tam-sam-som)). The current skill anchors SOM to competitor share or channel reach. Competitor share makes a useful cross-check, since a peer's share at a similar age shows what is achievable. It is not a build, because it ignores the startup's own capacity and the time needed to get there. Channel reach is closer to a build but needs a conversion rate and churn. For B2B, the build is ramped reps times quota times attainment, or channel reach times conversion times ACV, minus churn, summed over 36 months. For B2C, it is reach times funnel conversion times ARPU times retention. Telstra distribution is the classic muru-D trap: it should enter as a channel-reach input with its own stated conversion assumption, never as an assumed given. The benchmarks for sales capacity are weak. AE ramp-time figures attributed to the same Bridge Group research range from 4.7 to 7.2 months across secondary sources ([Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/cost-of-hiring-an-account-executive-2026); [Starr Conspiracy](https://www.thestarrconspiracy.com/insights/benchmarks/b2b-go-to-market-strategy-benchmarks-2024)), so the agent should range these inputs rather than pick one.

Two outside checks keep the build honest. The first is diffusion. A meta-analysis of 213 Bass-model applications gives pooled parameters of p = 0.03 and q = 0.38 ([Columbia Business School listing](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/meta-analysis-applications-diffusion-models)). Under those parameters a typical new category reaches only about **15% of its eventual potential by year 3**, summed across all vendors. That is close to Rogers' 16% for innovators plus early adopters ([CFI](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/learn/resources/economics/diffusion-of-innovation)). A startup's year-3 SOM is its share of that 15%, not of SAM. The parameters come mostly from consumer durables, so they are an order-of-magnitude prior, not a forecast. The second check is Damodaran's sum-of-shares test: list the named competitors' revenues, add the proposed SOM, and confirm the total fits inside the market. In 2015, the revenues implied by online-ad company valuations for 2025 added up to $523B, against an optimistic market of $466B ([Damodaran 2019](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2019/12/the-market-is-huge-revisiting-big.html?m=0)). This is the "big market delusion", and an agent that sizes one startup at a time will repeat it unless the test is built in.

Ranges should come from the drivers, not from the base case. Gurley ranged each driver at its own bounds: 3-6x expansion of car-for-hire, and 2.5-12.5% capture of car-ownership spend. He also warned that a few assumptions can move the answer by 25x ([Gurley 2014](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)). The agent should range buyer count, price, adoption and share at sourced bounds, compute the result in code, and report a text tornado that names the one or two inputs with the biggest swing. Multiplying every low and every high together gives an outer bound that is too wide. That is acceptable as long as it is labelled as an outer bound. LLM-stated ranges fail the other way: they are too narrow. Conformal widening of LLM intervals restored about 99% coverage and improved interval scores by 54% ([FermiEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26995)), so the widening should happen in code and never be left to the model. The 2x triangulation rule sits at the strict end of the 2-3x tolerance Stratrix recommends, which is fine. The weak point is what happens when the two methods disagree. A gap above tolerance means one estimate "contains a bad assumption" ([Stratrix](https://www.stratrix.com/craft/tam-sam-som)). The agent must name that assumption and fix it, and must never average the two.

## One letter cannot carry source quality, claim support and age

The current A-D scheme mixes three things in one grade: who published the figure, whether its sourcing is visible, and how old it is. A three-year-old ABS census figure and an unsourced report-mill number both land on C, although the census figure is far more trustworthy. The Admiralty Code, the long-standing intelligence standard, rates source reliability (A to F) separately from information credibility (1 to 6). It assesses each in isolation, so an unreliable source can still supply a confirmed fact ([Wikipedia: Admiralty code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiralty_code)). The agent should adopt that shape. Source grades run from S-A (ABS, ATO, regulators, filings) through S-B (an analyst with a disclosed method), S-C (a large report catalogue with no method shown) and S-D (wire copies, marketing, forums) to S-F (cannot judge). Claim grades run from C-1 (matched by an independent source) to C-5 (contradicted by a primary anchor), with C-6 for "cannot judge". The figure also records its data year separately from its publication date. GRADE adds one useful habit: each downgrade gets a named reason, such as indirectness (a global figure used for Australia), inconsistency, imprecision or vendor bias ([Cochrane Handbook ch. 14](https://www.cochrane.org/zh-hant/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-14)). A reviewer learns more from the reason than from the letter.

Report mills are the main threat to number quality, and an agent will find them first. Four 2025 estimates of the global digital health market range from **$199bn to $573bn**, almost 3x ([Sectorial](https://sectorial.io/resources/blog/market-research-providers-compared)). Mordor Intelligence published two UK reports with near-identical titles that gave $15.46bn and $42.93bn. Mordor also owns a majority of Global Market Insights, so the two firms' figures are not independent checks on each other ([Sectorial](https://sectorial.io/resources/blog/market-research-providers-compared)). One self-storage headline was 2.2x the census receipts for the same scope ([Hyperresearch](https://hyperresearch.ai/guides/how-to-check-a-market-research-report)). Both of these sources sell competing research tools, so their examples are illustrative rather than a systematic survey. Still, they show why majority voting backfires. GPT Researcher favours the most frequently reported figure ([GPT Researcher](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher)), and syndication makes a mill number the most frequent one. The treatment follows from this. Collapse syndicated copies and co-owned publishers into one independence group. Normalise metric type, scope, geography, year and currency before comparing any two figures. Exclude any figure that is more than 2x a primary anchor for the same scope. With five or more independent, comparable estimates, use a trimmed mean, since equal-weight combination cut forecast error by about 12.5% across 30 studies ([Armstrong 2001](https://repository.upenn.edu/marketing_papers/34)). Never average spend against revenue, or a global figure against a national one.

Provenance has to be recorded per number, not per document. Each figure needs a record with:

| Group | Fields |
|---|---|
| The number | value, unit, currency, metric type, geography, data year, scope |
| Where it came from | verbatim quote, URL, archive URL, publisher, published date, trace chain to the origin, independence group |
| How good it is | source grade, claim grade, downgrade reasons |

Derived numbers list their input IDs, formula and assumptions, which mirrors how W3C PROV links an entity to the activity that generated it ([CASRAI PROV-O](https://www.casrai.org/dictionary/term/prov-o)). This matters because citation failure is the best-measured LLM weakness. Over 60% of answers from eight AI search tools cited incorrectly ([CJR Tow Center](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/we-compared-eight-ai-search-engines-theyre-all-bad-at-citing-news.php)). Even the best deep-research system scored 90% citation accuracy ([DeepResearch Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11763v1)). The fix with the strongest evidence is a separate verification pass. It re-fetches each URL, checks that the quoted string appears on the page, and then asks a judge whether the quote supports the claim. That is how both benchmarks measure citation accuracy, and Anthropic runs a dedicated citation agent for the same reason ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). The Wayback availability API needs no authentication ([Internet Archive](https://archive.org/help/wayback_api.php)), so the archive link costs one call. If no snapshot exists, the agent should record "none" rather than block on saving one.

## DVF scores must cap at what desk research can prove

The current rubric's "5 = strong positive from A/B sources" confuses source quality with evidence strength. Strategyzer, which turned IDEO's DVF triad into an evidence-based system, rates evidence from 0 to 5. Interviews and surveys ("say" data) never score above 2, however many there are. Only "do" data reaches 3-5, and strength rises the closer the evidence gets to a real purchase, from a landing-page email up to a deposit or a mock sale ([Strategyzer, Luo 2021](https://strategyzer.com/library/business-testing-is-your-hypothesis-really-validated)). A credible publisher shows that a market exists. It does not show that these customers want this offer. The DVF skill should therefore split each lens into a signal (positive, mixed or negative) and an evidence level, with the level capped by evidence type:

| Evidence type | Max level |
|---|---|
| Desk research for Desirability or Viability | 2 |
| Third-party behaviour (reviews, competitor traction, public pricing) | 3 |
| Low-stakes do-data (fake door, qualified waitlist) | 3 |
| Commitments (LOI, deposit, paid pilot) | 4 |
| Repeat purchase at real price | 5 |

These caps are an adaptation of Strategyzer's scale to the agent's 1-5 range, not a published standard. Feasibility and Adaptability are different, because desk research can legitimately score high there. Component maturity, regulatory text and the competitor landscape are all public.

The rubric also lacks three things the reference scorecard treats as first-class. The Strategyzer Innovation Project Scorecard adds a fourth lens, **Adaptability**, covering industry forces, market forces, key trends and macro forces. It scores Strategic Fit and Opportunity size as separate axes and does not sum them into one number. It also places Channels and Customer Relationships under Desirability ([Strategyzer scorecard](https://www.csulb.edu/sites/default/files/document/innovation_project_scorecard.pdf)). This was the only robust public DVF scorecard found. Bayer adapted it for its innovation teams ([Strategyzer, Viki 2019](https://strategyzer.com/library/innovation-project-scorecard-evidence-trumps-opinion)), and no Telstra or Australian accelerator scorecard is public. Stage-Gate adds the idea of binary must-meet knockouts that sit beside weighted scores ([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/project-management/stage-gate-process)). For muru-D, that means Telstra strategic fit becomes a pass/fail gate, and Opportunity is anchored in AUD to the TAM/SAM/SOM output. Viability benchmarks must match the business model. Skok's rules are LTV:CAC above 3 and CAC payback under 12 months ([Skok](https://www.forentrepreneurs.com/saas-metrics-2/)), yet the 2025 median payback was about 20 months ([Aleph](https://www.getaleph.com/answers/cac-payback-period-saas-2026)). Median software gross margin was 80% ([Aleph](https://www.getaleph.com/answers/saas-gross-margin-2026)), while early AI-native "Supernovas" averaged about 25% ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/have-ai-gross-margins-really-turned-the-corner-the-real-math-behind-openais-70-compute-margin-and-why-b2b-startups-are-still-running-on-a-treadmill)). A blanket margin bar would wrongly fail marketplaces, hardware and AI-heavy ideas. For a pre-revenue idea, these ratios are always modelled and should be labelled as assumptions.

A weakest-lens verdict is defensible, because one fatal flaw kills an idea, but it has to run on confidence as well as score. A low score with weak evidence means "unknown" and leads to Pilot to test. A low score with strong evidence means "known bad" and leads to Rethink or Park. A failed must-meet gate, or a confidently negative Desirability result, means Park. Pursue should not be reachable from desk research alone. While no lens has do-evidence, the verdict caps at Pilot to test. The riskiest-assumption list already uses importance times evidence, which matches Bland's assumptions mapping and its test of importance: "which hypothesis, if proven wrong will cause our business idea to fail" ([Strategyzer, Bland 2020](https://strategyzer.com/library/how-assumptions-mapping-can-focus-your-teams-on-running-experiments-that-matter)). It needs testable form. Each assumption should take the "We believe that..." format, carry a D/V/F/A tag, and come with a Test Card: the metric, a pass threshold set before the test, and the evidence level the experiment would reach. Published benchmarks for experiments are practitioner rules of thumb only. One example: a CTA click-through above 10% is strong and below 3% is weak ([echai.ventures](https://echai.ventures/startingup/validate-idea/how-to-run-a-landing-page-fake-door-test)). One case shows the risk: 2,847 sign-ups drove a funding decision, and only 3% converted at launch ([Future Foundry via echai](https://echai.ventures/startingup/from/future-foundry)). The agent should label its thresholds as internal defaults and prefer B2B LOIs and pilots, which Telstra's channels make cheap to run.

The scoring pass also needs protection from the model's own tilt. In one study, 14 of 16 models leaned optimistic, and self-debiasing prompts did not remove the tilt ([OptimismBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26981)). In merchant-risk assessment, LLM judges scored an average of 0.46 points above expert consensus ([arXiv 2602.05110](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.05110)), and they also favour longer proposals ([arXiv 2604.22517](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.22517)). The agent should score from the extracted evidence ledger rather than from the founder's pitch text. It should also use elimination prompts ("which lens most likely kills this?"), which beat Likert ratings by about 33% for human raters screening ideas ([HKBU IIB](https://www.comp.hkbu.edu.hk/~iib/2015/Dec/article2/iib_vol16no1_article2.pdf)). Disagreement across several samples should lower confidence. Without anchored descriptors, individual rating items reach only about 0.59-0.66 rater agreement ([HAL-HEC](https://hal-hec.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02953039)), so every level needs a written anchor.

## Confidence needs a fixed vocabulary and a decision threshold

The current High, Medium and Low labels have no stated basis. IPCC practice derives confidence from evidence (limited, medium or robust) and agreement (low, medium or high). Evidence counts as robust only with "multiple, consistent independent lines of high-quality evidence" ([IPCC AR5 guidance](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/users/aldous/157/Papers/ipcc_uncertainty-guidance-note.pdf)). The agent can compute confidence the same way from the ledger: the number of independent groups, the spread ratio, the grades and how recent the data is. When there is no usable evidence, the output should say "unknown: insufficient evidence" plainly and not use a middle word. ICD 203, the US intelligence analytic standard, adds three rules. Likelihood comes from one fixed vocabulary with percentage bands. Confidence and likelihood never appear in the same sentence. Every key judgement names its causes of uncertainty and the indicators that would change it ([ICD 203](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)). The numbers matter. In a 924-person study, readers matched the intended probability bands 66% of the time when numeric ranges were bracketed in the text, against 32% without them ([PMC6469752](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6469752/)). So "likely (55-80%)" beats "likely". The agent must not mix ICD and IPCC scales, because their "likely" bands conflict.

Each headline figure should therefore read like this: a range with a base case, the data year and currency, a confidence label with its one-line basis, and a "would change if" line. Each report also needs ICD 203's source summary statement, which names the one to three sources that drive the headline, their grades, and the biggest weakness in the source base. The most decision-relevant addition borrows GRADE's idea of imprecision. Analysts care whether the range crosses their threshold. If the whole range clears the venture-scale bar, a wide range does not matter. If it straddles the bar, the report should say so in its first line. The common practitioner bar of a $1B+ TAM is unverified ([Pitch Deck Guide](https://pitchdeckguide.com/tam-sam-som-pitch-deck/)), so muru-D should set its own bar. Blackbird's partners say a standalone TAM/SAM/SOM slide "doesn't add value" because every deck shows big numbers, and that they look for a "big, hair on fire problem for a large number of customers" ([Overnight Success](https://www.overnightsuccess.vc/p/what-blackbird-wants-in-an-early-stage-pitch-deck)). That supports leading with the unit line and problem evidence, with TAM kept as context.

## Prioritised changes to the skills and pipeline

The order below puts first the changes that stop wrong numbers from reaching analysts. Changes that sharpen judgement come next, and polish comes last.

| # | Change | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Per-number ledger record (value, unit, currency, metric type, geography, data year, quote, URL, archive, independence group, source and claim grades, downgrade reasons). Reject any number without a verbatim quote from a fetched page. | Pipeline, Source Grading | Citation failure rates run 10-60% in current tools |
| 2 | All arithmetic, FX, CPI roll-forward, ranges and interval widening happen in the code runner from ledger IDs. The model never types a computed number. | Pipeline, Market Sizing | LLM intervals cover about 65% at nominal 99% |
| 3 | Separate verification pass: re-fetch each URL, string-match the quote, then have a judge check support. Drop or flag failures. | Pipeline | Strongest-evidence mitigation |
| 4 | Remove adoption from TAM. TAM = buyers x units x realized price. Adoption moves into SOM. | Market Sizing | Hides a judgement inside TAM |
| 5 | SOM = capacity or channel build over 36 months net of churn, cross-checked against Bass year-3 adoption (about 15%), a peer's share at the same age, and the sum of competitor shares within SAM. | Market Sizing | Share-anchored SOM has no mechanism |
| 6 | Split the A-D grade into a source grade (S-A to S-F) and a claim grade (C-1 to C-6), with named downgrade reasons and the data year shown. | Source Grading | One letter mixes who, sourcing and age |
| 7 | Separate DVF signal from evidence level. Cap the evidence level by type (desk research max 2 for D and V). Cap the verdict at Pilot to test when no do-evidence exists. | DVF Assessment | "5 from A/B sources" overstates proof |
| 8 | Verdict matrix that uses confidence: low and unknown leads to Pilot, low and known leads to Rethink, gate fail leads to Park. | DVF Assessment | Weakest-lens alone conflates unknown with bad |
| 9 | Anchoring guard: run the bottom-up build in a fresh context that excludes report-mill headlines, and only then compare. On a gap above 2x, name and fix the diverging input. Never average. | Pipeline, Market Sizing | Anchoring resists prompt mitigation |
| 10 | Driver-based low/base/high with a text tornado of the top two drivers, plus a threshold-crossing line. | Market Sizing | Blanket ranges hide what matters |
| 11 | Confidence computed from evidence x agreement. Use ICD 203 likelihood terms with numeric brackets, a "would change if" line and a source summary statement. | All three skills | Current labels have no basis |
| 12 | Add the Adaptability lens, a Telstra strategic-fit gate and an AUD Opportunity axis tied to SOM. Use margin benchmarks by business model and TRL for deep tech. | DVF Assessment | Matches the only robust public scorecard |
| 13 | Assumptions in "We believe that..." form with a D/V/F/A tag and a Test Card (metric, pre-set threshold, cost, target evidence level). | DVF Assessment | Lets the verdict map to a next gate |
| 14 | Separate the Expansion TAM line from TAM today for new categories, each with its own confirming evidence. | Market Sizing | Avoids both the Damodaran and the inflation error |
| 15 | Australian defaults: ABS 8165 employing businesses by ANZSIC and size band, AUD, financial year, and RBA FX at the source year's average rate. Treat HES-based consumer spend as low confidence. | Market Sizing, Research Method | 65% of ABS businesses are non-employing |
| 16 | Score DVF from the ledger, not the pitch text. Use an elimination prompt plus several samples, and treat disagreement as lower confidence. Run a fresh-context bear-case pass. | Pipeline | LLM judges are lenient and sensitive to framing |
| 17 | Evaluation set: about 20 ANZ markets with primary-source sizes, with the answer domain blocklisted. Score log10 error, interval coverage, quote-match rate and optimism gap. | Pipeline | Anthropic found about 20 queries enough to see large effects |

Two limits apply to all of this. No public muru-D or Telstra scoring criteria were found, so the gates and thresholds need sign-off from analysts. The experiment pass thresholds, the 1.5x and 3x spread heuristics, and the evidence caps are adapted or internal defaults, not published standards.

## Conclusion

The agent does not mainly need more search. It needs a structure that makes weak numbers visible. Every surveyed open-source research pipeline cites as it writes and checks nothing, and the one documented fix, Anthropic's separate citation pass, verifies sources rather than numbers ([GPT Researcher](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). A ledger with quote-verified inputs and code-computed outputs would therefore be muru-D's real differentiator. It also changes the analyst's job from recomputing figures to challenging the two or three inputs that the tornado shows matter most.

The deeper shift is that an honest first-pass report from a desk-research agent should usually return "Pilot to test" with low-to-medium confidence on Desirability and Viability. That is a correct statement of what the web can prove, not a weakness of the agent. Its value lies in naming the riskiest assumption and the cheapest experiment that would lift it to evidence level 3 or 4. A pipeline that often says "Pursue" on desk research alone is showing the same optimism tilt and score clustering that make existing idea validators untrustworthy ([BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/validatorai-alternative)).
